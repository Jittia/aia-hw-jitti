Moat Momentum

Strengthening moats, found in the filings.

Calm, precise and built on evidence, in the manner of a serious financial newspaper. This page sets the one look used by every output: web page, Deep-Dive pages, Google Sheet, Slides, Docs and HTML exports. We use our own name, mark and layout. The only thing we borrow is the idea of a warm “salmon paper” background.

1. Logo

The wordmark is the name set in Source Serif 4 semibold. Beside it sits a small abstract mark: a ring (the moat) around a line that rises (the momentum), ending in a teal dot (today). There are no mascots and no stock imagery.

Accessibility check (WCAG AA)

Normal text needs a contrast of at least 4.5:1. Large text, lines and chart marks need at least 3:1. I measured every pair. 5. Charts

Charts sit on the paper background with thin gridlines. Navy is the company, dashed grey is the industry median, and teal is a highlight such as the spread above the hurdle. Every chart has three things:

  • a title that states the takeaway;
  • the units;
  • a source line.

Lines are labelled directly, so no legend is needed.

7. Tone of voice

Do

  • “Gross margin rose from 41.2% to 46.8% over FY2021–FY2025; industry median 38.0%.”
  • Give a number, a period, a comparison and a source for every claim.
  • Say where the signal did not work, with the numbers.
  • Use plain English, short sentences and the same word for the same thing.

Don’t

  • “The next 10-bagger!”, “guaranteed”, “can’t lose”, “explosive”, “hidden gem”.
  • Make a claim with no number, or a number with no source.
  • Personal advice (“you should buy”). The output is the same for every user.
  • Jargon without a link to the Learn tab.

8. Do and don’t: visuals

Do

Revenue grew faster than the industry in 4 of 5 years

One message per chart. Navy for the company, grey for peers, thin gridlines and a source line.

Don’t

🚀 MOAT ROCKET 🚀

No hype type, gradients, emoji, rainbow series, 3D charts, stock photos or mascots. Never use red or green for decoration.
